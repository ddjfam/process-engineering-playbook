# Wells Fargo: Process Architecture and Repeatable Capability

**Experience summary | Group Process Architect contribution**

**Delivered:** 120 service models supporting the risk and control self-assessment (RCSA) program, plus BPMN-based training and a knowledge check for the Business Process Management team.

## Business problem

The RCSA rollout needed consistent process architecture. Leadership's view of risk-assessable units had to connect to the work performed by front-line teams, with clear ownership and a common modeling method.

## What I owned

I translated the top-down organizational view and bottom-up operating detail into sequenced BPMN process models. I worked with unit owners, process owners, business experts and front-line risk partners to develop a usable, shared process view.

I also built a training course and knowledge check so other team members could produce service models using a consistent approach. The goal was repeatable capability across the team, not dependence on one modeler.

## Delivered result

- **120 service models** delivered to support the RCSA rollout and regulatory remediation.
- A common modeling approach connecting business activities, ownership and risk assessment.
- Training materials that supported consistent execution by the BPM team.

These are delivered capability and process-architecture outcomes. This case does not claim a cash saving or sole ownership of the enterprise RCSA program.

## Relevance to CoE leadership

The transferable practices are shared standards, owner participation, training and quality review. My [CoE charter](../coe-charter-and-governance.md) extends those practices into a proposed operating model covering intake, prioritization, delivery and benefits governance.
