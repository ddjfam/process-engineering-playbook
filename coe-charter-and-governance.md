# Reengineering CoE: Charter, Team and Decision Rights

**Proposed operating model | Reusable portfolio toolkit**

## Mandate

Identify operational leakage, redesign end-to-end processes and help business owners realize measurable financial, customer and control outcomes across functions and locations. The CoE provides common methods, delivery support and independent challenge to benefit assumptions.

## Accountability

| Decision or responsibility | Accountable role | Supporting roles |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise priorities and funding | Executive sponsor / steering group | CoE lead, Finance, business leaders |
| Process outcome, local adoption and sustainment | Business process owner | Local leaders, CoE, change partner |
| Discovery standards, prioritization recommendations and quality gates | CoE lead | Process engineers, analysts, risk partners |
| Solution architecture, platform capacity and production release | Technology owner | Workflow lead, security/control specialists, business owner |
| Benefit classification, baseline and recognition | Finance partner with business owner | CoE analyst |
| Control design and acceptance | Accountable business/control owner | Risk specialists, technology, CoE |

The CoE can recommend investment, staffing and process changes. Spending, headcount and policy exceptions follow delegated authority. Unresolved cross-functional trade-offs go to the sponsor with options, consequences and a recommendation.

## Proposed core team

A starting model is a CoE lead, process engineer, data/benefits analyst and workflow business analyst. Technology engineering, change management, Finance and risk provide named shared capacity. Confirm actual people, skills and time commitments before setting delivery dates; adjust staffing to the approved pipeline.

## Governance routines

| Routine | Output |
|---|---|
| Weekly intake triage | Accepted, deferred or redirected problems; owner and next action |
| Weekly delivery and exception review | Blockers, dependencies, decisions and recovery actions |
| Monthly steering review | Portfolio priorities, capacity trade-offs, funding decisions and benefits status |
| Post-release benefit review | Finance/business validation; adoption, customer and control performance |
| Quarterly standards review | Reusable patterns, training needs and lessons incorporated into the toolkit |

## Gates and required evidence

1. **Accept the problem:** named owner, scope, customer impact and baseline data plan.
2. **Approve the design:** validated root causes, options, future-state map, controls and business case.
3. **Approve the pilot:** requirements, test cases, resources, adoption plan and rollback approach.
4. **Scale or stop:** pilot results, exception performance, customer feedback and benefit evidence.
5. **Transfer to operations:** owner accepts controls, support, metrics and sustainment reviews.

Urgent operational or control issues receive immediate escalation and containment rather than waiting for normal portfolio ranking.

## Standardization across locations

Define one common process and metric dictionary. Record necessary local variation with a reason, owner, control assessment and review date. Use local champions, short decision logs and asynchronous status updates to support distributed teams. Measure customer and process outcomes by location before assuming that one solution works everywhere.
